The center of mass was defined as a collection of point masses. We can extend these to a rigidbody by moving from a discrete description to a more continuous one

$$\vec{OG} = {\int_{vol} \vec r dm \over M}$$

>[!Warning] The gravitational force will always act at the center of mass of an object

>[!Tip] 
>It is possible to determine the center of mass in a more straightforward manner than integrating
>1. If the solid has an axis of symmetry, then the center of mass is on that axis
>2. If the solid has multiple axes of symmetry, then the center of mass is at their intersection

## Specific Solids

For two dimensional objects -> disks and plates <- we write $M = \rho_S \times S$
For one dimensional objects -> bars <- we write $M = \rho_l \times l$

## Composite Objects

We find the center of mass at 
$$\vec {OG} = {m_1 \vec {OG_1} + m_2 \vec{OG_2} \over m_1 + m_2}$$
for the combination of two objects

## Solid with a Hole

Just like a composition, but we remove the hole as if it were a solid *cork*
$$\vec{OG} = {m_2 \vec{OG_2} - m_1 \vec{OG_1} \over M}$$
