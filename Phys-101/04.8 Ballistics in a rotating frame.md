There are two ways to solve for the deviation caused by a rotating referential. Let's complete an example using the earth's rotation.
# Intuitive way

We assume that the object has an initial speed caused by the referential's rotation. 
$$\begin{array}l \text{object:} & \vec v_i &=&(R+h)\omega \vec e_y \\ \text{ground:} & \vec v_s &=& R\omega \vec e_y \\ & t_{fall} &=& \sqrt{\frac{2h}g} \end{array}$$
We have the displacements vectors of $\vec D_{object}$ and $\vec D_{ground}$
$$\begin{array}l \vec D_{object} &=& \vec v_it_{fall}&=&(R+h)\omega\sqrt{\frac{2h}g}\vec e_y \\ \vec D_{ground} &=&\vec v_s t_{fall} &=& R\omega \sqrt{\frac{2h}g}\vec e_y \end{array}$$
We therefore have the deviation of the object given by
$$\vec D = \vec D_{stone} - \vec D_{ground} = h\omega\sqrt{\frac{2h}g}\vec e_y$$
# Complete way

We have a frame $\mathcal R(O,x,y,z)$ fixed to the center of the earth and a frame $\mathcal R'(A,x',y',z')$ fixed to the point where we drop the object. 

We have Newton's second law in $\mathcal R$:
$$\sum \vec F = m\vec a_{\mathcal R}(P)=m\vec g$$
The earth's rotation vector is the same in both $\mathcal R$ and $\mathcal R'$ 
$$\vec\omega=\omega\vec e_z=\omega\vec e_{z'}$$
