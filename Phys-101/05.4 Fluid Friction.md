#phys

The fluid friction force depends on the geometry of the affected object as well as its velocity. We have two cases :

# Small velocities (or laminar regime)

The dependency is linear
$$\vec F_F = -b_l\vec v$$
With
- $b_l = K\eta$ : $\eta$ is the viscosity of the fluid, $K$ the shape factor

## Projectile

A projectile arrives with $\vec v_0$ in a fluid of viscosity $\eta$ and $K$ the shape coefficient. Ignore the effect of weight. (no grav)
Let's derive
$${dv_x \over dt} = -{K\eta\over m}v_x$$
We set $\lambda = K\eta/m$ and $v_x(0)=v_0$. We find
$$v_x(t)=v_0e^{-\lambda t}$$
## Object

An object is dropped without any velocity and is subjected only to its weight $\vec P = m\vec g$ 
$${dv_z\over dt}={1\over m}(mg-b_lv_z)$$
We call *the terminal velocity* the velocity $\vec v_\lim$ at which $\vec P = \vec F_F$ 
$$v_\lim = {mg\over b_l}$$
# High velocities (or turbulent regime)

The dependency is quadratic
$$\vec F_F = -b_tv^2{\vec v \over v}$$
With
- $b_t$ : a constant
- $v = \Vert \vec v \Vert$ 


>[!Remark]
>Most of the time it isn't either one of the cases, but a mix of both.
