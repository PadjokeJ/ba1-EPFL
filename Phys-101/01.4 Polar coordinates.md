They are very useful for uniform circular motion (UCM)
## Example : 

since it is circular motion, $\rho$ is a constant!
since it is uniform, $\phi = \omega t$, where $\omega$ is also constant
then $\dot \rho = \ddot \rho = 0$, $\dot \phi = \omega$, $\ddot \phi = 0$  

>[!tip]
>Always write the general formula and then simplify it for the exercise

$\overrightarrow v = \not \!\rho \overrightarrow e_\rho + \rho \dot \phi \overrightarrow e_\phi = R \omega \overrightarrow e_\phi$ -> tangent to the circle
$\overrightarrow a = - \rho \dot \phi ^2 \overrightarrow e_\rho = -R \omega ^2 \overrightarrow e_\rho$ (it is negative, therefore acceleration is oriented towards the center of the circle, we call this centripital acceleration) -> $a = R\omega ^2 \implies a = \frac{v^2}{R}$
