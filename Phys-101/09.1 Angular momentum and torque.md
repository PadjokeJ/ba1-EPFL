Angular momentum characterizes the rotation of an object around a point $O$. 
Torque -- or moment of a force -- makes it possible to characterize the ability of a force to produce rotational motion.

For a point $P$ of mass $m$, momentum $\vec p$ and the external force $\vec F$, we can define two things :
1. The angular momentum around point $O$ $$\overrightarrow{L_O} = \overrightarrow{OP} \wedge \vec p$$
2. The Moment of the force $\vec F$ around point $O$ $$ \overrightarrow{M_O} = \overrightarrow{OP}\wedge \vec F$$
   If there are multiple forces -> $\vec F_{tot} = \sum_i \vec F_i$ $$\overrightarrow{M_O} = \sum_i \overrightarrow{M_O^i} = \sum_i \overrightarrow{OP} \wedge \vec F_i$$
We know that $\vec F={d\vec p \over dt}$, therefore we can differentiate the equation according to time
$${d \vec L_O \over dt} = \frac d{dt}(\vec{OP} \wedge \vec p) = \vec{OP} \wedge \frac{d \vec p}{dt} = \vec{M_O}$$
-> ${d \vec{L_O} \over dt} = \vec{M_O}$
