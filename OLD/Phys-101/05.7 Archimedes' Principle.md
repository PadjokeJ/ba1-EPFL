#phys

A body immersed inside of a fluid is subject to an upward force equal to the weight of the fluid's displaced volume $m_f = \rho V$, with $\rho$ being the density of the fluid. The force is represented
$$\vec F_A = -\rho V\vec g$$

When an object is falling into a liquid, it is subject to gravity, the fluid friction and the upward buoyancy. The terminal velocity is achieved when all these forces cancel out.
$$m\vec g + \vec F_F + \vec F_A = \vec 0 \quad \iff \quad -mg -b_lv_\lim + \rho Vg=0$$
We find the terminal velocity 
$$v_\lim = {\rho Vg -mg\over b_l}$$
