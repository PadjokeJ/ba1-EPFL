We usually find ourselves inside a center of mass reference frame. 
In this frame, the velocities are colinear and in opposite directions. 

The distance $b$ between the two direction vectors of the particles is called the impact parameter. 

>[!Info] When $b=0$, we have a **head on collision**

>[!check] Obviously, if $b \gt (R_1 + R_2)$, then there is no collision

## Head on collision

When the collision is elastic, the momentum and kinetic energies are both conserved. 
$$\begin{array}c 
\vec P_{\text{before}} & = & \vec P_{\text{after}} \\
E_c^{\text{before}} & = & E_c^{\text{after}}
\end{array}$$

>[!Info] Notation
>The instant right after the impact is denoted as $'$

$$\begin{array}c
\vec P_\text{tot} & = & \vec P_1 + \vec P_2 & = & 0 \\
\vec P^{'}_\text{tot} & = & \vec P^{'}_1 + \vec P^{'}_2 & = & 0
\end{array}$$
We find that the velocities 
$$\begin{array}c 
\vec V_1 = -\vec V_1^{'} \\
\vec V_2 = -\vec V_2^{'} \\
\end{array}$$

If we return to the Galilean reference frame $\mathcal R$, we find
$$\begin{array}c
\vec v_1 &=& {(m_1 - m_2) \cdot \vec v_1 + 2m_2\vec v_2\over m_1 + m_2} \\
\vec v_2 &=& {(m_2 - m_1) \cdot \vec v_2 + 2m_1\vec v_1\over m_1 + m_2}
\end{array}$$
