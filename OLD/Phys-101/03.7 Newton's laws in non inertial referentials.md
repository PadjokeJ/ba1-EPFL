We have seen that the Newtonian laws are only valid in Galilean reference frames. Therefore $$\sum\vec F_{ext} = m \cdot \vec a = m \cdot \vec a_{\mathcal R}(P)$$
## But what if we are in an [[02.2 Accelerated reference frames|accelerated reference frame]]?
We want to observe from inside the accelerated reference frame, so we have to translate the galilean referential formulas into our referential.

We can use the formula we have deduced in the previous chapter to find our movement in the current accelerated reference frame.$$m\vec a_{\mathcal R'}(P) = m \vec a_{\mathcal R}(P) - m\vec a_{\mathcal R}(A) - m \vec \omega \wedge (\vec \omega \wedge \vec{AP}) - m \cdot 2 \vec \omega \wedge \vec v_{\mathcal R'} (P)$$
We can rewrite this using the formula we defined atop this document: $$m\vec a_{\mathcal R'}(P) = \sum \vec F_{ext} - m\cdot\left(\vec a_{\mathcal R} (A) + \vec \omega \wedge (\vec \omega \wedge \vec{AP}) + 2 \vec \omega \wedge \vec v_{\mathcal R'}(P) \right) $$
We can break these down into two different forces:
- the Coriolis force
- the driving force (also called transport force)
