When an object is placed onto a surface, the atoms of the two come closely together and have a significant interaction. It is an electromagnetic force, but we model them as **the normal force** and **the force of friction**

# Normal force

The normal force is always perpendicular to the surface, and points away from the surface.
It is denoted either with $\vec R$ or $\vec N$

>[!Example]
>![[Pasted image 20251004092800.png]]
>In this example, a mass is placed on a horizontal plane. The mass is at rest, so $m\vec a = \sum \vec F = \vec 0$, but we have the force of gravity $\vec P = m\vec g$ acting on the weight. It therefore cancels out with the normal force $\vec R$
>$$m\vec g + \vec R = \vec 0 \implies \vec R = -m\vec g$$

However, not all planes are horizontal.

>[!Example]
>![[Pasted image 20251004093226.png]]
>Here, a mass is placed on a support placed at an angle $\alpha$. Since the mass remains stuck on the plane, we have $v_y = 0$. The weight $\vec P = m\vec g$, and the normal force have the following vectors
>$$\vec R \left|\begin{array}c 0\\ R\gt 0 \end{array}\right.,\quad m\vec g \left|\begin{array}c mg\sin\alpha \\-mg\cos\alpha \end{array}\right.$$
>Since we know the mass is not flying of the plane $a_y = 0$, we get
>$$R = mg\cos\theta$$
