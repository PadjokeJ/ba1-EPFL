#phys 

It depends on both the geometry of an object, as well as its specific axis of rotation

| Type                                           | Formula                                                   |
| ---------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| Simple rod with equidistant masses $m$         | $$I_\Delta = \frac12 ml^2$$                               |
| Thin hollow cylinder, around its axis          | $$I_\Delta = M R^2$$                                      |
| A thin rod of mass $M$                         | $$I_\Delta = \frac1{12} M L^2$$                           |
| Sphere, around an axis                         | $$I_\Delta = \frac25 M R_S^2$$                            |
| Rectangular parallepiped, around its center    | $$I_\Delta = \frac1{12}M (a^2 + b^2)$$                    |
| Filled cylinder, around the axis of the circle | $$I_\Delta = \frac12 M R^2$$                              |
| Filled cylinder, around the wrong axis         | $$I_\Delta = \frac14 M \left( R^2 + \frac{h^2}3 \right)$$ |
>[!Theorem] Steiner's theorem
>When an object for which the axis is translated
>$$I_{O_z} = I_{G_z} + m l'^2$$
>where $l'$ is the translated distance
