This time the oscillator is under fluid friction from the air

The friction force is written
$$\vec F_f = -b_l\vec v = -b_l\dot x\hat e_x= -(K\eta)\dot x\hat e_x$$
The force is always in the opposite direction as the motion, which is why it is negative.

We find
$$\ddot x + 2\gamma\dot x + \Omega_0^2x=0,\quad 2\gamma = \frac{b_l}m=\frac{K\eta}m$$
The first step to solve this equation is to solve
$$\lambda^2+2\gamma\lambda+\Omega_0^2=0$$
Which we find by replacing $\ddot x$ and $\dot x$ with $\lambda^2,\lambda^1,\lambda^0=1$
We have
$$\Delta= b^2-4ac = (2\gamma) ^2 - 4 (1) (\Omega_0^2)$$
We can then find three cases : 
- Critical Damping ($\Delta = 0,\gamma=\Omega_0$)
$$x(t)=(A+Bt)e^{-\gamma t}$$
- Strong Damping ($\Delta \gt 0, \gamma^2\gt\Omega_0^2$)
  With 2 solutions : 
$$x(t)=Ae^{\lambda_1t}+Be^{\lambda_2t}$$
- Under-damped Regime ($\Delta\lt 0, \gamma^2\lt\Omega_0^2$)
  With the solution of the discriminant being complex!
  In this case we have $\lambda_{1,2}=-\gamma\pm iw$

These 3 cases behave differently

The critical damping case returns to the rest position rather instantly
The strong damping case doesn't oscillate
The under-damped regime case has a pseudoangular frequency and a pseudo period of oscillation
