#phys 
Friction is another force caused by electromagnetic interactions. It opposes to motion.

We have two types of friction:
- dry friction (a solid onto another)
- fluid friction (within fluids)

Here we will talk about dry friction, which has two forms

# Form 1: static friction

This happens when an object is stationary -> $\sum \vec F = \vec 0$
The force of friction $\vec F_F$ is preventing and "perfectly" compensates a force trying to set the object in motion. This obviously has a cap (try pushing something that is around you, you will be succesful). We call this cap "the coefficient" of static friction, and denote it as $\mu _s R$ 

We therefore have that $F_F \le \mu_sR$ 

# Form 2: dynamic/kinetic friction

This is a force applied when a body is in motion.
$F_F$ is colinear and directed away from the velocity of the object. We once again have a coefficient of friction, this time being for kinetic friction, denoted $\mu_c$

We have $F_F = \mu_c R$


>[!Tip] Remark
>We have $\mu_s \gt \mu_c$ most of the time
