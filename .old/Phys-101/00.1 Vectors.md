#phys
Characterized by its *magnitude*, *direction* and *orientation*  
These are usually forces, movements, speeds and accelerations

With two vectors $\overrightarrow{AB}$ and $\overrightarrow{BC}$, their sum will be equal to $\overrightarrow{AC}$ 
Often we will represent vectors with a single letter, such as $\overrightarrow{u}$, $\overrightarrow{v}$ or $\overrightarrow{w}$ .  
The vector object is independent from its point of origin, so when adding two vectors, we can move them to be aligned correctly for the addition : example, for the sum of the forces.
The 0 vector is represented by $\overrightarrow{0}$   
The last important characteristic is the point of application.  
With basic trigonometry we can determine a vector's horizontal and vertical components, aka cartesian components
$$\overrightarrow{a} \implies a_x, \;a_y, \;a_z$$
$\overrightarrow{AB} = \overrightarrow{OB} - \overrightarrow{OA}$, where $O$ is the point of origin

## Magnitude

the magnitude of $\overrightarrow{AB}$ is written $[AB]$ 
$\|\overrightarrow{a}\| := \sqrt{a_x^2 \;+\; a_y^2 \;+\; a_z^2}$ 
We can also write it $\|\overrightarrow{a}\| = a$ 

### Dot product 

$\overrightarrow{OA} \bullet \overrightarrow{OB} = \|\overrightarrow{OA}\|*\|\overrightarrow{OB}\| * cos(\theta)$ , where $\theta$ is the angle between $\vec{OA}$ and $\vec{OB}$ 
> [!Info] See [[00.2 Trigonometry]] for more info on cos, sin and angles

If we project OB on OA perpendicularly B', therefore the dot product is the product of ||OB'|| and ||OA||
if $\vec{u}$ is perpendicular to $\vec{v}$, then the dot product will be 0
if co-linear with $\vec{v}$, then the dot product is $u*v$ 
## Cross product
$\vec{u}\wedge\vec v = \vec w$ 
the vector $\vec w$ is orthogonal to $\vec u$ and $\vec v$ 
its magnitude is $w=u*v*sin(\theta)$  

The trick is to write them in order, $e_x, e_y, e_z, e_x, e_y$, to get their cross product, ie $e_x \wedge e_y = e_z$ 

For x, you cross the line of x, and mult $a_y * b_z$ - mult $a_z * b_y$, etc but for y is negative => ==CROSS== product
$c_x = a_y * b_z - a_z * b_y$
$c_y = -a_x * b_z + a_z * b_x$
$c_z = a_y * b_x - a_x * b_y$
