## What is it?

It is a declarative sentence that you can answer by only true or false. <- this is a proposition

$x + 3 = 7$ is not a proposition unless we are provided $x$ 

## Notation

- We use variables with a single letter name to hold propositions -> $p$, $q$. $r$, $s$, etc...
- **T** means true, **F** means false

### Compound propositions
- Negation $\lnot$ not
- Conjunction $\land$ and
- Disjunction $\lor$ or
- Implication $\to$ implies
- Biconditional $\leftrightarrow$ implies both ways
### Declaration

$a := \text{ the earth is round}$
$\lnot a$ would be that the earth is not round (can also be written $\bar a$)

### Truth tables

>[!example]- Example for a conjunction:
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \land \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | ---------------- |
>| T   | T   | T                |
>| T   | F   | F                |
>| F   | T   | F                |
>| F   | F   | F                |

>[!example]- Example for a disjunction:
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \lor \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | --------------- |
>| T   | T   | T               |
>| T   | F   | T               |
>| F   | T   | T               |
>| F   | F   | F               |

> [!Info] In english there is not difference between the inclusive or $\lor$ and the exclusive or $\oplus$ 

>[!example]- Example for the exclusive or
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \oplus \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | ----------------- |
>| T   | T   | F                 |
>| T   | F   | T                 |
>| F   | T   | T                 |
>| F   | F   | F                 |


#### Implication !

> [!Warning] Not very intuitive

It is true if the first is false, and if both are true

>[!example]- Example for implication
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \to \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | -------------- |
>| T   | T   | T              |
>| T   | F   | F              |
>| F   | T   | T              |
>| F   | F   | T              |

can be represented by $(a \land b) \lor \lnot a$

> [!Warning] Don't think about what the proposition says, just think about the truth value (T or F)

>[!Tip] 
>In english: $p \text{ if } q$ 

#### Biconditionality $\leftrightarrow$ 

$p$ if and only if $q$ 

it is true if they have the same truth value

>[!Example]- Example for biconditionality
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \leftrightarrow \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | -------------------------- |
>| T   | T   | T                          |
>| T   | F   | F                          |
>| F   | T   | F                          |
>| F   | F   | T                          |
