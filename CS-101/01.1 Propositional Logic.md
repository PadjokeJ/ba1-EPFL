## What is it?

It is a declarative sentence that you can answer by only true or false. <- this is a proposition

$x + 3 = 7$ is not a proposition unless we are provided $x$ 

## Notation

- We use variables with a single letter name to hold propositions -> $p$, $q$. $r$, $s$, etc...
- **T** means true, **F** means false

### Compound propositions
- Negation $\lnot$ not
- Conjunction $\land$ and
- Disjunction $\lor$ or
- Implication $\to$ implies
- Biconditional $\leftrightarrow$ implies both ways
### Declaration

$a := \text{ the earth is round}$
$\lnot a$ would be that the earth is not round (can also be written $\bar a$)

### Truth tables

>[!example]- Example for a conjunction:
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \land \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | ---------------- |
>| T   | T   | T                |
>| T   | F   | F                |
>| F   | T   | F                |
>| F   | F   | F                |

>[!example]- Example for a disjunction:
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \lor \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | --------------- |
>| T   | T   | T               |
>| T   | F   | T               |
>| F   | T   | T               |
>| F   | F   | F               |

> [!Info] In english there is not difference between the inclusive or $\lor$ and the exclusive or $\oplus$ 

>[!example]- Example for the exclusive or
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \oplus \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | ----------------- |
>| T   | T   | F                 |
>| T   | F   | T                 |
>| F   | T   | T                 |
>| F   | F   | F                 |


#### Implication !

> [!Warning] Not very intuitive

It is true if the first is false, and if both are true

>[!example]- Example for implication
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \to \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | -------------- |
>| T   | T   | T              |
>| T   | F   | F              |
>| F   | T   | T              |
>| F   | F   | T              |

can be represented by $\lnot p \lor q$ 

> [!Warning] Don't think about what the proposition says, just think about the truth value (T or F)

>[!Tip] 
>In english: $p \text{ if } q$ 

#### Biconditionality $\leftrightarrow$ 

$p$ if and only if $q$ 

it is true if they have the same truth value  
can be written as $\lnot(p \lor q)$ 

>[!Example]- Example for biconditionality
>| $a$ | $b$ | $a\; \leftrightarrow \; b$ |
>| --- | --- | -------------------------- |
>| T   | T   | T                          |
>| T   | F   | F                          |
>| F   | T   | F                          |
>| F   | F   | T                          |

## Precedence

| operator          | precedence |
| ----------------- | ---------- |
| $\lnot$           | 1          |
| $\land$           | 2          |
| $\lor$            | 3          |
| $\to$             | 4          |
| $\leftrightarrow$ | 5          |

## Classification

Based on what the truth table result
### Tautology 

if it is always true
### Contradiction

if it is always false
### Contingency

If it is sometimes true and sometimes false (so neither [[#Tautology]] or [[#Contradiction]])
### satisfiable

if there can be a truth value of true (so if it is not a [[#Contradiction]])  
to determine wether a proposition is satisfiable, we only need to find one truth value

### logical equivalence

if $p \leftrightarrow q$ is a [[#Tautology]], then $p$ is logically equivalent to $q$, noted $p \equiv q$ 

#### common equivalences

are on the cheat sheet

##### identity laws

$p \land T \equiv p$
$p \lor T \equiv p$
##### domination laws

$p \lor T \equiv T$
$p \land F \equiv F$
##### Idempotent laws

$p \lor p \equiv p$
$p \land p \equiv p$
##### Double negation law

$\lnot (\lnot p)$
##### commutative laws

$p \lor q \equiv q \lor p$
$p \land q \equiv q \land p$
##### associative laws

$(p \lor q) \lor r \equiv p \lor (q \lor r)$
$(p \land q) \land r \equiv p \land (q \land r)$
##### distributive laws

$p \lor (q \land r) \equiv (p \lor q) \land (p \lor r)$


### Contrapositive, Converse and Inverse

| Statement             | Conditional    | Statement |
| --------------------- | -------------- | --------- |
| $q \to p$             | converse       | $p \to q$ |
| $\lnot p \to \lnot q$ | inverse        | $p \to q$ |
| $\lnot q \to \lnot p$ | contrapositive | $p \to q$ |

## Normal forms 

### Disjunctive Normal Form

a disjunction ($\lor$) of one or more conjunctions ($\land$)

to find it, show the complete truth table, and for the results where it is true, note the values of the variables

>[!Example]-
>
>| p     | q   | r   | \lnot r | p \lor q | (p \lor q) \to \lnot r |
>| ----- | --- | --- | ------- | -------- | ---------------------- |
>| T     | T   | T   | F       | T        | F                      |
>| T | T   | F   | T       | T        | T                      |
>| T     | F   | T   | F       | T        | F                      |
>| T     | F   | F   | T       | T        | T                      |
>| F     | T   | T   | F       | T        | F                      |
>| F     | T   | F   | T       | T        | T                      |
>| F     | F   | T   | F       | F        | T                      |
>| F     | F   | F   | T       | F        | T                      |
>
>here we take rows where it is true, and note down the values of p, q and r
### Conjunctive Normal form

a conjunction ($\land$) of one or more disjunctions ($\lor$)

to find find it, with the truth table we use the FALSE value of propositions
