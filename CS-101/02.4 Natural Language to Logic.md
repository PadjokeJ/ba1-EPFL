then split the sentence into propositions with predicates

WE USE MOSTLY IMPLICATION BECAUSE OUTSIDE OF THE FIRST CONDITION SHOULD BE TRUE

## Example

- $P(x)$: "x is a duck"
- $Q(x)$: "x is a police officer"
- $R(x)$: "x is a baby"
- $S(x)$: "x is willing to waltz"
 
- "No ducks are willing to waltz" -> $\forall x(P(x) \to \lnot S(x))$ OR $\lnot \exists x (P(x)) \land S(x))$ 
- "some babies are willing to waltz" -> $\exists x (R(x) \to S(x))$ 
- "No police officer will ever decline to waltz" -> $\lnot \exists (Q(x) \to S(x))$
